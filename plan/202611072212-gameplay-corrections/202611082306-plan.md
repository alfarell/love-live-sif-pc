## H3: note size and bracket spacing

- **Current state:**
  - `kNoteSize` = 0.95 × 256, so the ring arrives at about 0.80 of the indicator ring;
  - brackets are drawn at 1.17 × the note quad in every state.
- **Expected state:**
  - the ring at arrival is 0.87-0.93 of the indicator ring, as in the game;
  - the gap between bracket and ring matches the game (about 0.10 of the ring);
  - proportions are kept in all nine lanes.
- **Identified issue:** in the game the bracket-to-ring ratio is 1.22 on held notes but 1.41-1.46 in flight. The port uses 1.42 everywhere. This must be measured before choosing constants.
- **Affected code:**
  - `Playfield::kNoteSize`;
  - the bracket factor in `RunLive` (to become a named constant);
  - possibly `Path()`, if the game's growth curve differs;
  - a measurement script under `work/gameplay-fix/` (not committed).
- **Steps:**
  1. Write a script that finds the ring and bracket edges along the centre row of a note (alpha-free colour edges). Run it on the shadPS4 run 1 frames, at least three in-flight pairs, two held pairs and two lone notes at the hit, and on port captures at the same song and time.
  2. Fit the note scale at arrival. Fit the size-vs-progress curve too, if the in-flight points do not lie on the port's 0.2 → 1.0 line.
  3. Fit the bracket factor separately for in-flight and held notes. If the two fits differ, use two constants, and record the reason as a *Hypothesis*.
  4. Set the constants and recapture.
- **Test:**
  - the script's ratios for the port are within ±3% of the game's;
  - a visual pass at song 0010 14 s and 30 s, with simultaneous pairs, holds and the centre-lane → □ icons;
  - icons stay centred.
- **Regressions:**

## H4: brackets at simultaneous hold tails

- **Current state:** brackets only on heads. `GroupNotes` numbers groups by head time.
- **Expected state:**
  - hold tails that end together also wear brackets, around their end caps;
  - a group's tail colour is the opposite of its head colour;
  - over consecutive groups the colours follow the user's table: every hold-pair group goes head orange, tail blue;
  - tap pairs keep alternating orange and blue.
- **Rule:** rule A from the analysis.
  - Collect events (heads at `time_ms`, hold tails at `end_ms`) and sort them by time.
  - Events within 1 ms that have two or more members form a group.
  - Number the groups 0, 1, 2… and colour each by its number % 2.
- **Assets:** brackets (3, 3) and (4, 3) of `ui_ingame_notes`.
- **Affected code:**
  - `src/game/live/chart.{h,cpp}`: add `Note::end_group` (-1 if none); rewrite `GroupNotes`; `MarkIcons` calls it as now;
  - `SetBands`: store the tail's group in `Tail`;
  - `DrawBands`: draw the tail bracket after the cap;
  - `tests/unit/live_test.cpp`: `Groups()`.
- **Steps:**
  1. Change `GroupNotes` to the event rule.
  2. Draw `bracket[end_group % 2]` at the tail with H3's factor, while the tail is on screen.
  3. Decide whether a held tail keeps its brackets. Image 4 shows them kept.
- **Test:**
  - unit cases: two hold pairs in a row give heads 0 and 2 with tails 1 and 3; a tap pair between them shifts the colours; a lone tail has no group; old tap-only charts are numbered as before;
  - a capture of a held pair against image 4 and frame `0100_026224`.
- **Open:** the three cases the analysis marks as uncertain. Check them in a shadPS4 run if one turns up. Until then use rule A and label it a *Hypothesis*.

## H5: judgement sounds

- **Current state:**
  - no sound effects;
  - `AudioOutput` has one voice, and `Play()` replaces it;
  - `LoadCue` decodes one waveform per cue.
- **Expected state:**
  - each judgement plays the game's cue: 20030-20034 normally, 20039-20043 (`_fl`) once the finale is on;
  - all tracks of the cue are mixed;
  - the music clock is unchanged.
- **Assets:** `sound/sound.pac/sys.acb`, cues 25-29 (`Perfect`, `Great`, `Good`, `Bad`, `miss`) and 32-36 (`…_fl`).
  - Waves: Perfect 184 + 249 + 46; Great 80 + 248 + 46; Good 79 + 247 + 46; Bad 76 + 246 + 46; Miss 204. The `_fl` cues add 195.
- **Mapping:** grade → cue is one to one, so five cues, not three. Perfect to Bad share the common layer 46.
- **Affected code:**
  - `engine/audio/audio_output.{h,cpp}`: a small fixed pool of sound-effect voices (for example 8) mixed in the callback after the music voice. `fed_frames_` and the clock keep counting the **music** voice only;
  - `engine/audio/cue_sheet.{h,cpp}` and `sound_bank.{h,cpp}`: list every waveform of a cue (`FindWaves`); `LoadCue` gains a way to mix them;
  - `src/app/live_screen.cpp`: preload the ten cues at live start; play from the judgement log loop;
  - unit test: `sif_audio_test`.
- **Steps:**
  1. Decode the ten cues' waves to WAV under `work/audio/` (asking first) and listen. Note which sound alike, for the user's "about three".
  2. Decode the undecoded track commands of these sequences (volume or delay?). If they cannot be read, mix all tracks at full volume and record that as a shortcut.
  3. Build the voice pool: lock-free hand-off from the main thread, mixing with a clamp, no allocation in the callback.
  4. Pick the cue at `Judge::Log` time: grade → table, column 1 if `judge.finale` and the note is at or after `finale_ms`.
  5. **Duplicates:** in one frame, play each cue at most once. Two notes judged Perfect together give one Perfect sound. *Hypothesis:* until the game's caller of `0xB06C0` is traced.
  6. **Hold release:** play the release judgement's cue too, and flag it as a *Hypothesis*.
  7. **Volume:** use the master volume. The options screen has no sound-effect volume yet.
- **Test:**
  - unit: the mixer returns music frames unchanged when no effect plays; the clock's fed frames do not change when effects play; cue resolution returns the expected wave ids for cues 25-29;
  - manual: autoplay song 0010 and listen; miss on purpose to hear Miss.
- **Regressions to watch:**
  - timing: compare `audio_offset_ms` calibration and judgement offsets before and after;
  - CPU cost in the callback;
  - clipping when many sounds overlap;
  - menus that call `Play()` (top-menu voice, `--play`) still work.
- **Open:** the callers of `0xB06C0` (a function pointer); the meaning of `[ctx+0x60]+8 == 4`; the special-chart table `0x5E8B70` and the mash cue 21100; the finale-entry cue 87.
